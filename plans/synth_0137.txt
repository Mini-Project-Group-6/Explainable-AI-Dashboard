SUBJECT: Mathematics
TOPIC: Area of Plane Shapes
CLASS: JHS 2
DURATION: 70 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 30

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- demonstrate a curriculum indicator for Area of Plane Shapes using at least three evidence
- classify the key idea of formula in one lesson
- construct square with a clear example

PCK:
Content is accurate throughout, anticipates common misconceptions for formula, and includes pitched worked examples.

RESOURCES:
a ruler and measuring tape; a projector where power is available; chalkboard and chalk.

INTRODUCTION:
Teacher opens with a short hook to settle the class. Teacher reviews learners' previous knowledge of rectangle. This is connected to today's topic, Area of Plane Shapes, and in particular to formula. The objective is shared with learners at the start.

CORE POINTS:
Area of Plane Shapes involves formula. Learners should note that formula is a key point of today's lesson.
Area of Plane Shapes involves square. Learners should note that square is a key point of today's lesson.
Area of Plane Shapes involves length. Learners should note that length is a key point of today's lesson.
Area of Plane Shapes involves triangle. Learners should note that triangle is a key point of today's lesson.
Area of Plane Shapes involves breadth. Learners should note that breadth is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
(20 minutes) Learners role-play the process of formula in front of the class.
(15 minutes) Learners draw and label a chart of square in their exercise books.
(15 minutes) Learners draw and label a chart of length in their exercise books.
(20 minutes) Teacher demonstrates triangle using TLMs while learners observe and ask questions.
(15 minutes) Teacher reads the section on breadth from the textbook.

EVALUATION:
1. Calculate the answer to the exercise on formula on the board.
2. Describe how square occurs.
3. Explain the role of length in your own words.
4. List 4 examples of triangle.
5. What is meant by breadth?

DIFFERENTIATION:
Learners with special educational needs are seated in front and given adapted materials. Slower learners receive targeted support during the group work.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses an analogy, diagram, and demonstration from the Ghanaian classroom context to explain formula.

SEQUENCING:
Starter, main, and plenary all present with coherent progression; each activity builds on the previous; timings are realistic and balanced. Phase timings are shown throughout.

CLOSURE:
The main points are consolidated on the board. Learners summarise in their own words what they have learnt. Teacher asks two questions to confirm attainment before dismissal.
