SUBJECT: Mathematics
TOPIC: Fractions
CLASS: JHS 3
DURATION: 70 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 53

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- list a curriculum indicator for Fractions using correctly evidence
- explain the key idea of addition in one lesson
- construct simplify with a clear example

PCK:
Content is accurate and appropriate to grade level with worked examples for addition.

RESOURCES:
chalkboard and chalk; a labelled diagram of simplify.

INTRODUCTION:
Teacher opens with a short hook to settle the class. This is connected to today's topic, Fractions, and in particular to addition.

CORE POINTS:
Fractions involves addition. Learners should note that addition is a key point of today's lesson.
Fractions involves simplify. Learners should note that simplify is a key point of today's lesson.
Fractions involves denominator. Learners should note that denominator is a key point of today's lesson.
Fractions involves equivalent fractions. Learners should note that equivalent fractions is a key point of today's lesson.
Fractions involves numerator. Learners should note that numerator is a key point of today's lesson.
Fractions involves addition. Learners should note that addition is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
(20 minutes) Learners role-play the process of addition in front of the class.
(10 minutes) In groups, learners investigate simplify and record their observations.
(20 minutes) Learners draw and label a chart of denominator in their exercise books.
(20 minutes) Teacher explains equivalent fractions to the class.

EVALUATION:
1. What is meant by addition?
2. What is meant by simplify?
3. Calculate the answer to the exercise on denominator on the board.
4. What is meant by equivalent fractions?
5. Explain the role of numerator in your own words.

DIFFERENTIATION:
Key terms are explained in the local language where needed. A challenge question is provided for the most able. Learners with special educational needs are seated in front and given adapted materials.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses an analogy, diagram, and demonstration from the Ghanaian classroom context to explain addition.

SEQUENCING:
Starter, main, and plenary all present with coherent progression; each activity builds on the previous; timings are realistic and balanced. Phase timings are shown throughout.

CLOSURE:
The main points are consolidated on the board.
