SUBJECT: Integrated Science
TOPIC: Photosynthesis
CLASS: JHS 3
DURATION: 60 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 26

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- state a curriculum indicator for Photosynthesis using using the chart provided evidence
- demonstrate the key idea of carbon dioxide in one lesson
- solve leaf with a clear example

PCK:
Content is accurate throughout, anticipates common misconceptions for carbon dioxide, and includes pitched worked examples.

RESOURCES:
chalkboard and chalk; real objects collected from the school compound.

INTRODUCTION:
Teacher opens with a short hook to settle the class. Teacher reviews learners' previous knowledge of plants. This is connected to today's topic, Photosynthesis, and in particular to carbon dioxide. The objective is shared with learners at the start.

CORE POINTS:
Photosynthesis involves carbon dioxide. Learners should note that carbon dioxide is a key point of today's lesson.
Photosynthesis involves leaf. Learners should note that leaf is a key point of today's lesson.
Photosynthesis involves glucose. Learners should note that glucose is a key point of today's lesson.
Photosynthesis involves oxygen. Learners should note that oxygen is a key point of today's lesson.
Photosynthesis involves chlorophyll. Learners should note that chlorophyll is a key point of today's lesson.
Photosynthesis involves sunlight. Learners should note that sunlight is a key point of today's lesson.
Photosynthesis involves plants. Learners should note that plants is a key point of today's lesson.
Photosynthesis involves carbon dioxide. Learners should note that carbon dioxide is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
(20 minutes) In groups, learners investigate carbon dioxide and record their observations.
(10 minutes) Learners discuss leaf in small groups and present their findings to the class.
(10 minutes) In groups, learners investigate glucose and record their observations.
(15 minutes) Through question and answer, learners brainstorm what they know about oxygen.
(20 minutes) Teacher explains chlorophyll to the class.

EVALUATION:
1. Calculate the answer to the exercise on carbon dioxide on the board.
2. Explain the role of leaf in your own words.
3. Explain the role of glucose in your own words.
4. Describe how oxygen occurs.

DIFFERENTIATION:
Learners may first discuss in their mother tongue.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses an analogy, diagram, and demonstration from the Ghanaian classroom context to explain carbon dioxide.

SEQUENCING:
Starter, main, and plenary all present with coherent progression; each activity builds on the previous; timings are realistic and balanced. Phase timings are shown throughout.

CLOSURE:
The main points are consolidated on the board. Learners summarise in their own words what they have learnt. Closing questions check whether the objective was met.
