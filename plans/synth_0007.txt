SUBJECT: Mathematics
TOPIC: Fractions
CLASS: JHS 3
DURATION: 60 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 36

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- construct a curriculum indicator for Fractions using at least two evidence
- describe the key idea of addition in one lesson
- compare simplify with a clear example

PCK:
Content is accurate throughout, anticipates common misconceptions for addition, and includes pitched worked examples.

RESOURCES:
a ruler and measuring tape; chalkboard and chalk; a wall chart showing equivalent fractions; a projector where power is available.

INTRODUCTION:
Learners recall what they already know about numerator. This is connected to today's topic, Fractions, and in particular to addition. The objective is shared with learners at the start.

CORE POINTS:
Fractions involves addition. Learners should note that addition is a key point of today's lesson.
Fractions involves simplify. Learners should note that simplify is a key point of today's lesson.
Fractions involves equivalent fractions. Learners should note that equivalent fractions is a key point of today's lesson.
Fractions involves denominator. Learners should note that denominator is a key point of today's lesson.
Fractions involves numerator. Learners should note that numerator is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
(20 minutes) Learners role-play the process of addition in front of the class.
(15 minutes) Learners draw and label a chart of simplify in their exercise books.
(20 minutes) Learners role-play the process of equivalent fractions in front of the class.
(15 minutes) Through question and answer, learners brainstorm what they know about denominator.
(20 minutes) Teacher explains numerator to the class.

EVALUATION:
1. Explain the role of addition in your own words.
2. Explain the role of simplify in your own words.
3. List 4 examples of equivalent fractions.
4. What is meant by denominator?
5. List 3 examples of numerator.

DIFFERENTIATION:
Learners may first discuss in their mother tongue. Slower learners receive targeted support during the group work. An extension task is ready for learners who finish early. Participation is shared fairly between girls and boys.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses familiar examples to explain addition clearly.

SEQUENCING:
Starter, main, and plenary all present with coherent progression; each activity builds on the previous; timings are realistic and balanced. Phase timings are shown throughout.

CLOSURE:
Learners summarise in their own words what they have learnt.
