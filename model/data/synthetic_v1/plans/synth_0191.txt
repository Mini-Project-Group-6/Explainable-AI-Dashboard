SUBJECT: Integrated Science
TOPIC: The Water Cycle
CLASS: JHS 3
DURATION: 36 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 50

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- know evaporation

PCK:
Content is accurate throughout, anticipates common misconceptions for evaporation, and includes pitched worked examples.

RESOURCES:
a whiteboard marker for key terms; counters for the group task.

INTRODUCTION:
Teacher reviews learners' previous knowledge of condensation. This is connected to today's topic, The Water Cycle, and in particular to evaporation.

CORE POINTS:
The Water Cycle involves evaporation. Learners should note that evaporation is a key point of today's lesson.
The Water Cycle involves rivers. Learners should note that rivers is a key point of today's lesson.
The Water Cycle involves rainfall. Learners should note that rainfall is a key point of today's lesson.
The Water Cycle involves clouds. Learners should note that clouds is a key point of today's lesson.
The Water Cycle involves precipitation. Learners should note that precipitation is a key point of today's lesson.
The Water Cycle involves condensation. Learners should note that condensation is a key point of today's lesson.
The Water Cycle involves evaporation. Learners should note that evaporation is a key point of today's lesson.
The Water Cycle involves rivers. Learners should note that rivers is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
Teacher explains evaporation to the class.
Learners draw and label a chart of rivers in their exercise books.
Teacher writes notes about rainfall on the board for learners to copy.
Learners practise solving exercises on clouds in pairs.
Through question and answer, learners brainstorm what they know about precipitation.

EVALUATION:
1. Explain the role of evaporation in your own words.
2. Describe how rivers occurs.
3. Describe how rainfall occurs.

DIFFERENTIATION:
Key terms are explained in the local language where needed. An extension task is ready for learners who finish early. Groups are mixed ability so learners support one another.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses familiar examples to explain evaporation clearly.

SEQUENCING:
A phase is missing and timings are absent.

CLOSURE:
Teacher summarises the key points of the lesson. Learners state the key points to the class. Teacher asks two questions to confirm attainment before dismissal.
