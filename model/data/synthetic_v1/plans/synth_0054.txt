SUBJECT: Mathematics
TOPIC: Area of Plane Shapes
CLASS: JHS 2
DURATION: 80 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 55

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- explain a curriculum indicator for Area of Plane Shapes using without referring to the textbook evidence
- evaluate the key idea of breadth in one lesson
- demonstrate length with a clear example

PCK:
Content is accurate and appropriate to grade level with worked examples for breadth.

RESOURCES:
a phone image of breadth; flashcards for length; a ruler and measuring tape.

INTRODUCTION:
Teacher opens with a short hook to settle the class. Learners recall what they already know about square. This is connected to today's topic, Area of Plane Shapes, and in particular to breadth. The objective is shared with learners at the start.

CORE POINTS:
Area of Plane Shapes involves breadth. Learners should note that breadth is a key point of today's lesson.
Area of Plane Shapes involves length. Learners should note that length is a key point of today's lesson.
Area of Plane Shapes involves triangle. Learners should note that triangle is a key point of today's lesson.
Area of Plane Shapes involves rectangle. Learners should note that rectangle is a key point of today's lesson.
Area of Plane Shapes involves formula. Learners should note that formula is a key point of today's lesson.
Area of Plane Shapes involves square. Learners should note that square is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
(20 minutes) Through question and answer, learners brainstorm what they know about breadth.
(20 minutes) Teacher reads the section on length from the textbook.
(10 minutes) Teacher demonstrates triangle using TLMs while learners observe and ask questions.
(10 minutes) Through question and answer, learners brainstorm what they know about rectangle.

EVALUATION:
1. Explain the role of breadth in your own words.
2. What is meant by length?
3. Describe how triangle occurs.
4. List 2 examples of rectangle.

DIFFERENTIATION:
Key terms are explained in the local language where needed. Participation is shared fairly between girls and boys.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses an analogy, diagram, and demonstration from the Ghanaian classroom context to explain breadth.

SEQUENCING:
Starter, main, and plenary all present with coherent progression; each activity builds on the previous; timings are realistic and balanced. Phase timings are shown throughout.

CLOSURE:
The main points are consolidated on the board. Learners summarise in their own words what they have learnt. Teacher asks two questions to confirm attainment before dismissal.
