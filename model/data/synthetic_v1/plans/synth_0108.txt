SUBJECT: Integrated Science
TOPIC: States of Matter
CLASS: JHS 3
DURATION: 70 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 32

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- describe the main idea of liquid
- calculate solid

PCK:
Content is broadly correct but thin and generic for liquid.

RESOURCES:
worksheets on liquid.

INTRODUCTION:
Teacher opens with a short hook to settle the class. This is connected to today's topic, States of Matter, and in particular to liquid. The objective is shared with learners at the start.

CORE POINTS:
States of Matter involves liquid. Learners should note that liquid is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves solid. Learners should note that solid is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves melting. Learners should note that melting is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves boiling. Learners should note that boiling is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
(10 minutes) Teacher reads the section on liquid from the textbook.
(15 minutes) Through question and answer, learners brainstorm what they know about solid.
(15 minutes) Learners practise solving exercises on melting in pairs.
(10 minutes) In groups, learners investigate boiling and record their observations.
(15 minutes) Learners discuss particles in small groups and present their findings to the class.
(15 minutes) In groups, learners investigate gas and record their observations.

EVALUATION:
1. Describe how liquid occurs.
2. Explain the role of solid in your own words.
3. What is meant by melting?

DIFFERENTIATION:
Groups are mixed ability so learners support one another.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses familiar examples to explain liquid clearly.

SEQUENCING:
Starter, main, and plenary all present with coherent progression; each activity builds on the previous; timings are realistic and balanced. Phase timings are shown throughout.

CLOSURE:
Teacher summarises the key points of the lesson. Learners summarise in their own words what they have learnt.
