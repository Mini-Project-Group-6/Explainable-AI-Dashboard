SUBJECT: Integrated Science
TOPIC: States of Matter
CLASS: JHS 2
DURATION: 70 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 26

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- construct a curriculum indicator for States of Matter using at least two evidence
- evaluate the key idea of particles in one lesson
- demonstrate boiling with a clear example

PCK:
Content is accurate and appropriate to grade level with worked examples for particles.

RESOURCES:
flashcards for particles; chalkboard and chalk.

INTRODUCTION:
Teacher opens with a short hook to settle the class.

CORE POINTS:
States of Matter involves particles. Learners should note that particles is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves boiling. Learners should note that boiling is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves solid. Learners should note that solid is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves gas. Learners should note that gas is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves liquid. Learners should note that liquid is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves melting. Learners should note that melting is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
(10 minutes) Teacher writes notes about particles on the board for learners to copy.
(15 minutes) Learners draw and label a chart of boiling in their exercise books.
(10 minutes) Through question and answer, learners brainstorm what they know about solid.
(20 minutes) Through question and answer, learners brainstorm what they know about gas.
(10 minutes) Learners discuss liquid in small groups and present their findings to the class.

EVALUATION:
1. What is meant by particles?
2. List 3 examples of boiling.
3. Describe how solid occurs.
4. Calculate the answer to the exercise on gas on the board.

DIFFERENTIATION:
Provision is made for a learner with a hearing impairment. A support task with scaffolded steps is prepared for learners who struggle.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses familiar examples to explain particles clearly.

SEQUENCING:
Starter, main, and plenary all present with coherent progression; each activity builds on the previous; timings are realistic and balanced. Phase timings are shown throughout.

CLOSURE:
Lesson ends.
