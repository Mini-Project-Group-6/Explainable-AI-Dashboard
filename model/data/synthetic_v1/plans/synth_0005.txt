SUBJECT: Integrated Science
TOPIC: States of Matter
CLASS: JHS 2
DURATION: 60 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 52

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- state a curriculum indicator for States of Matter using with the aid of real objects evidence
- calculate the key idea of particles in one lesson
- list gas with a clear example

PCK:
Content is accurate throughout, anticipates common misconceptions for particles, and includes pitched worked examples.

RESOURCES:
specimens of particles; a whiteboard marker for key terms; a wall chart showing liquid.

INTRODUCTION:
Teacher opens with a short hook to settle the class. Learners recall what they already know about boiling. This is connected to today's topic, States of Matter, and in particular to particles. The objective is shared with learners at the start.

CORE POINTS:
States of Matter involves particles. Learners should note that particles is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves gas. Learners should note that gas is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves liquid. Learners should note that liquid is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves melting. Learners should note that melting is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves solid. Learners should note that solid is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves boiling. Learners should note that boiling is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves particles. Learners should note that particles is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves gas. Learners should note that gas is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
(10 minutes) Learners draw and label a chart of particles in their exercise books.
(20 minutes) Teacher demonstrates gas using TLMs while learners observe and ask questions.
(20 minutes) Teacher explains liquid to the class.
(15 minutes) Learners practise solving exercises on melting in pairs.
(10 minutes) Teacher demonstrates solid using TLMs while learners observe and ask questions.

EVALUATION:
1. What is meant by particles?
2. Explain the role of gas in your own words.
3. List 2 examples of liquid.
4. What is meant by melting?

DIFFERENTIATION:
Groups are mixed ability so learners support one another. A challenge question is provided for the most able.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses familiar examples to explain particles clearly.

SEQUENCING:
Starter, main, and plenary all present with coherent progression; each activity builds on the previous; timings are realistic and balanced. Phase timings are shown throughout.

CLOSURE:
Teacher summarises the key points of the lesson. Learners state the key points to the class.
