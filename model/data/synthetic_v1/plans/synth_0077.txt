SUBJECT: Integrated Science
TOPIC: States of Matter
CLASS: JHS 3
DURATION: 70 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 49

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- calculate the main idea of liquid
- describe particles

PCK:
Content is accurate throughout, anticipates common misconceptions for liquid, and includes pitched worked examples.

RESOURCES:
real objects collected from the school compound.

INTRODUCTION:
Teacher revises the previous lesson on boiling. This is connected to today's topic, States of Matter, and in particular to liquid. The objective is shared with learners at the start.

CORE POINTS:
States of Matter involves liquid. Learners should note that liquid is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves particles. Learners should note that particles is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves solid. Learners should note that solid is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves melting. Learners should note that melting is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves gas. Learners should note that gas is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves boiling. Learners should note that boiling is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves liquid. Learners should note that liquid is a key point of today's lesson.
States of Matter involves particles. Learners should note that particles is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
(10 minutes) Learners discuss liquid in small groups and present their findings to the class.
(20 minutes) Learners practise solving exercises on particles in pairs.
(20 minutes) Teacher writes notes about solid on the board for learners to copy.
(15 minutes) Learners practise solving exercises on melting in pairs.

EVALUATION:
1. Explain the role of liquid in your own words.
2. Describe how particles occurs.
3. Explain the role of solid in your own words.

DIFFERENTIATION:
Slower learners receive targeted support during the group work.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses an analogy, diagram, and demonstration from the Ghanaian classroom context to explain liquid.

SEQUENCING:
Starter, main, and plenary all present with coherent progression; each activity builds on the previous; timings are realistic and balanced. Phase timings are shown throughout.

CLOSURE:
Learners summarise in their own words what they have learnt. Closing questions check whether the objective was met.
