SUBJECT: Mathematics
TOPIC: Percentages
CLASS: JHS 1
DURATION: 60 minutes
CLASS SIZE: 30

OBJECTIVES:
By the end of the lesson, the learner will be able to:
- compare a curriculum indicator for Percentages using at least two evidence
- identify the key idea of percent in one lesson
- solve discount with a clear example

PCK:
Content is accurate and appropriate to grade level with worked examples for percent.

RESOURCES:
the class textbook; specimens of discount.

INTRODUCTION:
Teacher opens with a short hook to settle the class. Teacher reviews learners' previous knowledge of decimal. This is connected to today's topic, Percentages, and in particular to percent. The objective is shared with learners at the start.

CORE POINTS:
Percentages involves percent. Learners should note that percent is a key point of today's lesson.
Percentages involves discount. Learners should note that discount is a key point of today's lesson.
Percentages involves profit. Learners should note that profit is a key point of today's lesson.
Percentages involves fraction. Learners should note that fraction is a key point of today's lesson.
Percentages involves conversion. Learners should note that conversion is a key point of today's lesson.
Percentages involves decimal. Learners should note that decimal is a key point of today's lesson.

TEACHER-LEARNER ACTIVITIES:
(15 minutes) Learners role-play the process of percent in front of the class.
(15 minutes) Teacher reads the section on discount from the textbook.
(20 minutes) Through question and answer, learners brainstorm what they know about profit.
(15 minutes) Learners discuss fraction in small groups and present their findings to the class.

EVALUATION:
1. Calculate the answer to the exercise on percent on the board.
2. What is meant by discount?
3. Explain the role of profit in your own words.

DIFFERENTIATION:
No reference to differing learner needs.

EXPLANATION STRATEGY:
Teacher uses an analogy, diagram, and demonstration from the Ghanaian classroom context to explain percent.

SEQUENCING:
Starter, main, and plenary all present with coherent progression; each activity builds on the previous; timings are realistic and balanced. Phase timings are shown throughout.

CLOSURE:
Closing questions check whether the objective was met.
