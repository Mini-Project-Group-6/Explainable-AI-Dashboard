# Participant information sheet

## What is this study about?

Your college is using a dashboard that scores lesson plans against ten criteria
taken from the National Teachers' Standards. For each score, the dashboard also
explains *why* it gave that score — for example, by showing which parts of your
plan raised or lowered it.

We want to find out whether these explanations help student teachers trust the
feedback, and whether they help student teachers improve their lesson plans.

## Why am I being asked?

You are a student teacher at {{PARTICIPATING_COLLEGES}} with an account on the
dashboard. Every student teacher with an account is invited.

## Do I have to take part?

No. It is completely your choice.

If you say no, you can still use the dashboard in exactly the same way.
Nothing about your studies, your marks or your placement will be affected.
Your tutors will not see your survey answers, and they will not be told
whether you took part.

## What will I be asked to do?

- **A first survey, about 10 minutes**, before your first lesson plan is
  scored. It asks how useful and easy to use you expect the dashboard to be,
  and how far you expect to trust it. There are also two optional questions:
  your level, and how often you have used AI tools before.
- **Use the dashboard as you normally would.**
- **A follow-up survey, about 10 minutes**, after two more of your lesson
  plans have been scored. It repeats most of the first survey and asks what
  you thought of the explanations.

Both surveys are in the dashboard's **Trust survey** tab. There are no right
or wrong answers.

## What information will be collected?

- Your survey answers, and how long each survey took you.
- The scores the dashboard gives each lesson plan you upload, and when you
  uploaded it.

The dashboard does not keep your lesson plan files. Each file is deleted as
soon as it has been scored; only its file name and its scores are saved.

## How will my information be protected?

- When you agree to take part, you are given a random participant code (for
  example, P-3F9A12C4). The data the research team analyses carries only this
  code. It never includes your name, your email address or the names of your
  files.
- The link between your code and your account stays inside the dashboard's
  database, on {{STORAGE_LOCATION}}, behind password sign-in. It is kept only
  so that you can withdraw.
- Results will be reported only as group summaries, in theses, journal
  articles and conference presentations. Nobody will be identifiable from
  them.
- Your information will be kept for {{RETENTION_PERIOD}} and then deleted. It
  is handled in line with Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843).

## Can I change my mind?

Yes. You can withdraw at any time, without giving a reason, from the
**Trust survey** tab. Withdrawing deletes your survey answers immediately and
removes your scored plans from the research data. You can keep using the
dashboard afterwards.

If you withdraw after {{WITHDRAWAL_DEADLINE}}, your answers are still deleted
from the dashboard, but they may already be part of group results that have
been analysed, and those results cannot be changed.

If you say no at first, you can still join later from the same tab.

## Are there any risks?

The risks are small. The two surveys take about 20 minutes in total.

The dashboard's scores come from a computer model, and like any model it can
be wrong. If a score seems unfair, trust your own judgement and your tutor's.
The dashboard's scores are practice feedback. They are not part of your
assessment.

## Are there any benefits?

You get feedback on your lesson plans whether or not you take part. Taking
part helps us learn how to make AI feedback more useful for student teachers
in Ghana. {{COMPENSATION_STATEMENT}}
